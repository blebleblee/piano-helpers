## 1. The Physical Concept

Traditional sheet music requires you to constantly look back and forth between a page on your music stand and your hands on the keys. 

These cards work differently: **they sit directly behind your physical piano keys**, resting on the fallboard (the vertical ledge right behind the keys). 

```text
       ┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
       │             PIANO LEARNING HELPER CARD                 │
       │                                                        │
       │  [Table of Chords / Notes]                             │
       │  [Inversion Fingerings: 1 - 3 - 5]                     │
       │  [Drawn Piano Keys: C  C#  D  D#  E  F  F#  G ...]     │
───────┴────────────────────▼ ROOT ▼────────────────────────────┴─────── [90° Fold]
       ┌───┬───┬───┬───┬───┬───┬───┬───┬───┬───┬───┬───┬───┬───┬───┐
       │   │ █ │   │ █ │   │   │ █ │   │ █ │   │ █ │   │   │ █ │   │  [Real Piano Keys]
       │ C │C# │ D │D# │ E │ F │F# │ G │G# │ A │A# │ B │ C │C# │ D │
       └───┴───┴───┴───┴───┴───┴───┴───┴───┴───┴───┴───┴───┴───┴───┘
```

Because the columns on the card are calibrated to **13.79 mm per semitone**, each column aligns **1:1 with standard 88-key piano fallboards** (including the Roland FP-30X PHA-4 keyboard, Yamaha, Kawai, and acoustic grand pianos). 

When you look straight down from any note badge on the card, your eyes land directly on the physical piano key you need to press.

## 2. Printing & Setup

1. **Paper Format:** All cards are designed for standard **A4 paper in Landscape orientation** (297 × 210 mm).
2. **Print Settings (Crucial!):**
   * Select **100% Scale** (or "Actual Size").
   * **Do NOT** select "Fit to Printable Area" or "Shrink to Page". Scaling down will shrink the 13.79 mm column pitch and cause the card to misalign with your keyboard.
   * Safe margins (~12 mm) are already built into the design, so your printer will not cut off any columns.
3. **Folding the Base Stand:**
   * Along the bottom edge of the drawn piano keys, you will see a red dashed line:
     ```text
     ▼ FOLD FORWARD 90° HERE — KEYS REST DIRECTLY BEHIND PIANO KEYS ▼
     ```
   * Fold the bottom flap **forward 90 degrees**. This creates an "L-shaped" base stand.
   * Set the card behind your keys so the vertical face stands upright against your piano fallboard.

## 3. The Core Philosophy: Transposition by Sliding

All chords of the same type share the **exact same interval shape**:
* A **Major Chord** is always: Root $\rightarrow$ $+4$ semitones (Major 3rd) $\rightarrow$ $+3$ semitones (Perfect 5th).
* A **Minor Chord** is always: Root $\rightarrow$ $+3$ semitones (Minor 3rd) $\rightarrow$ $+4$ semitones (Perfect 5th).

Because of this, **Column 0 on the card is ALWAYS the Root of your chord**, no matter what key you want to play.

### How to Transpose in 3 Seconds:
1. **Find your Root Note:** Decide what chord you want to play (e.g., $E\flat\text{ maj}$).
2. **Slide the Card:** Slide the entire card horizontally until the red **`▼ ROOT ▼`** arrow on Column 0 points directly to that key on your piano (e.g., center the arrow over the $E\flat$ key).
3. **Read Across that Row:** Look down row **`Eb maj`** on the card. 
   * Column 0 points to $E\flat$ (Root).
   * Column 4 points to $G$ (3rd).
   * Column 7 points to $B\flat$ (5th).
4. **Play the Inversions:** The finger numbers at the bottom (`1-3-5`, `1-2-5`, `1-3-5`) tell you which fingers to use for each inversion right over those keys.

## 4. Anatomy of a Card: Every Element Explained

### The Header & Legend
* **Title:** Shows the chord type (e.g., `MAJOR CHORDS`, `DOMINANT 7th CHORDS`) and the active hand (`RIGHT HAND` or `LEFT HAND`).
* **Formula Subtitle:** Shows the scale degrees (e.g., $1 - 3 - 5 - \flat 7$) and semitone step formula (e.g., $[4 + 3 + 3]$).
* **Right Panel:** Contains the Card Legend, Tone Color Key, and Hand Diagram.

### Tactile Key Shapes (`○` White vs. `●` Black)
Pianists naturally memorize chords by their **physical hand shape**—how the keys feel under their fingers:
* **`○` = White Key** (wide, flat surface)
* **`●` = Black Key** (narrow, raised surface)

Each row has an instant visual summary next to its name:
* **`C maj` `○ ○ ○`:** All fingers rest on flat **white keys** (White — White — White).
* **`D maj` `○ ● ○`:** Your outer fingers are on **white keys**, while your middle finger reaches up to a raised **black key** ($F\sharp$).
* **`Eb maj` `● ○ ●`:** Your outer fingers rest on raised **black keys** ($E\flat, B\flat$), and your middle finger drops down to a **white key** ($G$).
* **`Gb maj` `● ● ●`:** All fingers rest on raised **black keys**.

### Tone Colors & Chord Degrees
Every active note badge is color-coded by its musical function:
* 🔴 **Crimson = Root ($1$):** The tonal foundation of the chord.
* 🟠 **Warm Amber = 3rd ($3$ or $\flat 3$) / 2nd ($2$) / 4th ($4$):** Determines if the chord is major, minor, or suspended.
* 🟢 **Emerald = 5th ($5$ or $\flat 5$ / $\sharp 5$):** The harmonic anchor.
* 🔵 **Royal Blue = 7th ($7$ or $\flat 7$) / 6th ($6$):** The color extension for 7th and extended chords.

### The 12-Row Chord Matrix
* Lists all 12 chromatic root keys ($C, D\flat, D, E\flat, E, F, G\flat, G, A\flat, A, B\flat, B$).
* **Ivory Badges (White background with colored border):** The note is played on a **physical white key**.
* **Ebony Badges (Dark charcoal background with luminous text):** The note is played on a **physical black key**.

### Zebra Tracking Rails
Black keys on a piano sit between white keys. To make it effortless to track your eyes down from a chord near the top of the card to the piano keys below:
* Columns that correspond to **black keys** (Cols 1, 3, 6, 8, 10, 13, 15, 17) have **subtle vertical gray rails**.
* When you see a note on a gray rail, your eyes follow the rail straight down to a black key without drifting sideways into adjacent white keys.

### Bold Octave Boundary
* A prominent vertical boundary line divides **Column 11 ($B$)** from **Column 12 (Octave $C$)**.
* This marks where the primary root octave ends and the upper register begins.

### Inversion Fingerings
Positioned directly above the drawn piano keys:
* Displays the exact finger numbers for **Root Position**, **1st Inversion**, and **2nd Inversion**.
* Standard finger numbering:
  * **1 = Thumb**
  * **2 = Index**
  * **3 = Middle**
  * **4 = Ring**
  * **5 = Pinky**

### The Piano Key Bed
* Drawn directly at the bottom edge of the card, resting against your real piano keys.
* Shows the natural white and black keys starting from $C$, giving you an immediate visual anchor.

## 5. How to Read & Play Inversions

A chord can be played in different "voicings" by moving the lowest note up an octave:

| Inversion | Lowest Note | RH Fingering (Triads) | LH Fingering (Triads) | What It Sounds Like |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Root Position** | Root ($1$) | **`1 — 3 — 5`** | **`5 — 3 — 1`** | Strong, grounded, fundamental |
| **1st Inversion** | 3rd ($3$) | **`1 — 2 — 5`** | **`5 — 3 — 1`** | Smooth, melodic, less heavy |
| **2nd Inversion** | 5th ($5$) | **`1 — 3 — 5`** (or `1-2-4`) | **`5 — 2 — 1`** | Open, fanfare-like, transitional |

On the card, each inversion row displays the exact finger numbers inside the active key slots. Notice how in the 1st Inversion, your right hand naturally uses **Finger 2 (Index)** instead of Finger 3 because the interval between the 3rd and 5th is narrower!

## 6. How to Use Scale & Mode Cards

Scale cards (`scales/`) follow the same Unified Matrix layout:
* **Rows:** All 12 chromatic root keys.
* **Badges:** Display the note name and its scale degree ($1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8\text{ve}$).
* **Fingering Strips at Bottom:**
  * **RH FINGERS:** Standard ascending scale fingering (e.g., Major: `1-2-3-1-2-3-4-5`, showing where the thumb crosses under).
  * **LH FINGERS:** Left-hand fingering (e.g., Major: `5-4-3-2-1-3-2-1`).

### The Modes of the Major Scale
The collection includes all primary church modes:
* **Ionian (Major):** The standard bright diatonic scale ($W-W-H-W-W-W-H$).
* **Dorian:** Minor with a raised, jazzy 6th ($1 - 2 - \flat 3 - 4 - 5 - 6 - \flat 7$). Classic Miles Davis / modal jazz sound.
* **Phrygian:** Minor with an exotic, dark $\flat 2$ ($1 - \flat 2 - \flat 3 - 4 - 5 - \flat 6 - \flat 7$). Spanish Flamenco and metal flavor.
* **Lydian:** Major with a dreamy raised $\sharp 4$ ($1 - 2 - 3 - \sharp 4 - 5 - 6 - 7$). Film score magic (*The Simpsons*, John Williams).
* **Mixolydian:** Major with a bluesy $\flat 7$ ($1 - 2 - 3 - 4 - 5 - 6 - \flat 7$). Blues, rock, and jam band staple.
* **Aeolian (Natural Minor):** The standard pure minor scale ($W-H-W-W-H-W-W$).
* **Locrian:** Half-diminished mode with $\flat 2$ and $\flat 5$.

## 7. How to Use Chord Progression Cards

Progression cards (`progressions/`) teach you how to connect chords smoothly without jumping your hands across the keyboard:

1. **Voice Leading:** Chords are voiced using inversions so common notes stay under the same fingers.
2. **Common Tone Stars (`★`):** At the bottom, sustained keys that are shared across multiple chords in the progression are highlighted with a star (`★`). Keep those fingers stationary while changing the other notes!
3. **Available Progression Cards:**
   * **`progressions_pop.pdf`:** The iconic 4-Chord Wonder ($I - V - vi - IV$) and Ballad form ($vi - IV - I - V$).
   * **`progressions_jazz.pdf`:** The quintessential Jazz Cadence ($\text{ii} - V - I$) and Turnaround ($I - vi - \text{ii} - V$).
   * **`progressions_classic.pdf`:** Pachelbel's Canon sequence ($I - V - vi - \text{iii} - IV$) and 3-chord Rock/Blues ($I - IV - V$).
   * **`progressions_blues_12bar.pdf`:** Complete 12-bar blues form with dominant 7th voicings and turnaround.
   * **`progressions_circle_of_fifths.pdf`:** Standard jazz circle chain ($\text{ii}7 - V7 - \text{Imaj}7 - \text{IVmaj}7 - \text{vi}7$).
   * **`progressions_jazz_rootless.pdf`:** Professional two-hand voicings (Bill Evans A & B rootless voicings for $\text{ii}-V-I$).

## 8. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Q: Why don't the drawn black keys at the bottom match my piano when I slide the card to D?
**A:** A piano keyboard is physically irregular: it has groups of 2 black keys, a gap ($E-F$), 3 black keys, and another gap ($B-C$). Because of this, white and black keys do not repeat identically when shifted by arbitrary semitones. 

The drawn keys at the bottom show the natural keyboard starting from **C**. When you slide the card to another key (e.g., $D$), **rely on the note badges and key shape dots (`○` / `●`) on that chord's row**:
* The badges tell you whether that specific note is on a white key (`○`) or black key (`●`).
* The column grid still points directly down to the exact physical keys on your piano.

### Q: What does `[4 + 3]` mean next to the chord name?
**A:** That is the interval formula in semitones (half-steps):
* Start at the Root.
* Move up **4 semitones** to find the 3rd.
* Move up **3 more semitones** to find the 5th.

### Q: My card doesn't fit on my piano fallboard. What should I do?
**A:** Check your fold line. The card is designed to fold forward 90° along the red dashed line right below the piano keys. The bottom flap rests flat on your piano shelf, while the vertical card face stands upright against the fallboard.
