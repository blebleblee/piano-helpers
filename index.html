How to Use Piano Learning Helpers

These printable reference cards sit directly on your piano fallboard behind the physical keys to visually guide your fingers.

1. The 3-Second Sliding Transposition

Because every chord of the same type shares identical semitone intervals, Column 0 is always your Root Note:

  1. Find the Root Note you want to play (e.g. D or E♭).
  2. Slide the card until the red ▼ ROOT ▼ arrow points directly to that key on your piano.
  3. Look across that chord's row (e.g. D maj or Eb maj) to see the exact notes and key types.
  4. Play the finger numbers shown in the inversion rows right above the keys!

2. What Do the Dots Mean? (Key Shapes: ○ White vs. ● Black)

Pianists memorize chords by their tactile physical shape under the fingers:

  • ○ = White Key (flat, wide key surface)
  • ● = Black Key (raised, narrow key surface)

Examples:

  • C maj [○ ○ ○]: All three fingers rest on flat white keys.
  • D maj [○ ● ○]: Outer fingers on white keys, middle finger reaches up to a raised black key (F#).
  • Eb maj [● ○ ●]: Outer fingers on raised black keys, middle finger drops down to a white key (G).
